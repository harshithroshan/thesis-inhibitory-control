# Childhood Emotional Abuse, Emotional Neglect, and Inhibitory Control

Undergraduate thesis, 2026. Department of Psychology, Ashoka University.
Supervised by **Dr Simantini Ghosh**.

**Research question.** Do childhood emotional abuse and emotional neglect
relate differently to inhibitory control in Indian emerging adults?

Inhibitory control is treated here as a behavioral index rather than a clinical
one: the capacity to withhold a prepared response, measured through reaction
times and error rates on a Go/No-Go task. Emotional abuse and emotional neglect
are forms of psychological maltreatment that need not involve physical harm,
and are therefore difficult for participants to identify and report.

The design is informed by the Dimensional Model of Adversity and
Psychopathology, which treats childhood adversity as having at least two
dimensions — threat, and deprivation of expected cognitive, social and
emotional input. Emotional abuse is treated as threat-based here, and
emotional neglect as deprivation-based.

## Method

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Design | Cross-sectional, correlational |
| Participants | Target sample of 150 Indian participants aged 18–25 |
| Measures | Traumatic Antecedents Questionnaire (TAQ) subscale scores; items prevalidated for cultural relevance |
| Task | Emotional Go/No-Go task with positive, negative and neutral distractors, recorded as reaction time and accuracy |
| Stimuli | Emotional picture sets prevalidated for valence and arousal |
| Software | PsychoPy, G\*Power (a priori power analysis) |

Previous findings have been inconsistent, which is partly a measurement
problem: most studies use emotionally neutral tasks, and most treat
maltreatment as a single cumulative score. Distinguishing the forms of
adversity, and testing inhibitory control under emotional distraction as well
as neutral conditions, are the two changes this study is built around.
